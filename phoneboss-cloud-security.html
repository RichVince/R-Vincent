The Phone Boss ULTD — production enquiry workflow security review and technical control validation.

Disclosure: Sanitized owner-led case study based on point-in-time command output and owner statements. Not an independent audit, certification, penetration test or assurance of ongoing effectiveness. Proposed risk scores and treatment targets require owner approval.

1. Formal risk assessment

Objective: preserve confidentiality, integrity and availability of business enquiries while reducing exposure from development artifacts and improving recovery readiness. Scope: website intake, Cloudflare Workers and D1, email delivery integration, Windows development endpoint and offline backup. Excludes payment systems, full supplier assurance, comprehensive application penetration testing and enterprise-wide ISMS assessment.

Architecture: customer browser → Cloudflare Worker → production D1 database and email delivery service. Local Wrangler development state is separate from the remote database. A production SQL export is stored under restricted Windows permissions, encrypted into an archive and copied to offline removable storage.

Method: likelihood (1 rare, 2 unlikely, 3 possible, 4 likely, 5 almost certain) multiplied by impact (1 negligible, 2 minor, 3 moderate, 4 major, 5 severe). Scores 1–4 low, 5–9 moderate, 10–16 high, 17–25 critical. Impact considers confidentiality, integrity, service interruption and customer trust. Inherent scores are estimates before the assessed safeguards; residual scores reflect observed safeguards and remaining uncertainties, not measured probabilities.

Scope clarification: The seven scenarios above (R01–R07) document an earlier, backup-focused technical review. A subsequent broader internal assessment records 15 distinct scenarios (R-001–R-015) spanning Cloudflare administrative access, OAuth credentials and permissions, logging and retention, customer-data exposure, backups and recovery, endpoint protection, secrets, configuration, supplier disruption, and deployment operations. The registers use different identifiers and boundaries and must not be added together or treated as duplicate independent findings.

The later workbook records an inherent qualitative score total of 229 and a proposed residual score total of 91 across its 15 scenarios, a 60.3% change in summed ordinal scores. This is not a measured reduction in incident likelihood or a claim of certification. The proposed residual ratings await individual owner approval and some control conclusions remain partial or untested.

The expanded assessment maps 36 selected ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A controls and 22 NIST CSF 2.0 categories. These are relevance mappings, not a complete Statement of Applicability, independent audit, or proof that every control is effective. Original technical evidence is retained privately pending item-by-item validation and redaction; the internal workbook and unredacted outputs are intentionally not published.

7. Ongoing monitoring and closure

Proposed cadence: weekly encrypted backup and hash verification; monthly review of backup age and delivery failures; quarterly access and key-custody review and isolated restore exercise. Track successful backups / scheduled backups, age of last verified backup, restore test success, overdue high-risk actions and delivery failures. Review the risk register after significant changes. No recurring monitoring history or formal risk acceptance is claimed.
